§ 05

Inside the models

Trained on NHANES, cross-validated across two survey cycles. All numbers shown as-is.

How to read these numbers

AUC stands for "Area Under the ROC Curve." It's a single 0.5–1.0 score that measures how well the model ranks sick people above healthy people across every possible cutoff. 0.5 = coin flip, 1.0 = perfect ranking. AUC is threshold-free, which is why it's used as the headline metric.

Accuracy, Precision, Recall and F1 all depend on the threshold you use to call someone "flagged." This app uses two thresholds per model, shown below as bands:

  • Moderate band — the screening trigger. Threshold is tuned to catch ~90% of true cases. High recall, at the cost of more false alarms.
  • High band — the referral trigger. Threshold is tuned to catch ~60% of true cases. Higher precision, so a "high" reading is more likely to be a real case.

Plain-English definitions:

  • Accuracy — of 100 predictions, how many were right (positive or negative)
  • Precision — of everyone flagged, what percentage actually had the condition
  • Recall — of everyone who had the condition, what percentage did the model flag
  • F1 — the harmonic mean of precision and recall (one number balancing both)

For a screening tool, recall matters more than accuracy: missing a sick person is worse than flagging a healthy one for a follow-up. That's why accuracy at the moderate band looks lower than at the high band — the model is doing its job of casting a wide net on the first pass.

§ 06

What this is not

Not a diagnosis

It flags a pattern; it does not confirm disease. Every high result should be followed by a proper clinical workup.

Not a substitute for blood tests

Quick mode is a heuristic. Clinical mode uses lab values you enter — a real lab report is always more reliable.

Not validated for everyone

Training data is US-based adults. Not validated for children, pregnancy, or people with existing chronic conditions.

Not a medical device

No FDA or CDSCO clearance. It is a screening aid — think of it like a smoke alarm, not a fire extinguisher.
